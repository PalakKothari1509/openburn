The recommended way to get an estimate. Pick a departure airport, an arrival airport, and an aircraft type — OpenBurn works out the flight distance and duration automatically and predicts fuel burn and emissions from the trained model. Use this unless you already know the exact distance/duration yourself (see Manual Estimate).

Manual Estimate (by Distance)

For when you already know the flight's distance (not just the airport codes) — e.g. a custom or non-airport route, or you want to override the auto-calculated distance/duration from the Estimate by Route tab. Only aircraft type and distance are required; duration and cruise altitude are optional and improve accuracy if you have them.

Train the Model on Your Flight Data

This is the step that teaches OpenBurn your airline's real fuel-burn numbers. Upload one or more CSV files of past flights that include actual fuel burned — the model learns a separate pattern for each aircraft type from this data. You must train the model at least once before the Estimate by Route, Manual Estimate, or Bulk Estimate tabs will return a result. New uploads are added to everything trained before (e.g. uploading February's data keeps January's too), unless you check "Start fresh" to discard old data first.
